French law is hard to read: a single rule is spread across a statute, its decrees, tax annexes and future versions that often contradict one another. We report a proof of concept in which AI agents rewrite five major French codes in full: the Labour Code, the General Tax Code and its four annexes, the Social Security Code, the Town Planning Code, and the Construction and Housing Code, 30,317 articles from the official LEGI database as of 18 October 2026. The work ran in three passes and an open public review. A first pass rewrote every article (14,115 rewritten, 16,199 retained with a justification); a second re-read all of them for contradictions; a third, run as an independent red team, raised 303 objections, applied about 1,290 corrections and measured reliability with blind random audits; public GitHub issues fed the same loop. On two fresh random samples totalling 1,264 articles, audited blind on both texts with every disputed defect checked, the rewritten law has 54% fewer gross defects than the law in force (95% CI 40--64%): AI auditors find one in 3.5% ± 1.0 of rewritten articles against 7.5% ± 1.5 of official ones (paired test, p ≈ 2×10⁻¹⁰). The rewrite repaired 60 defects of the official law, mostly dead cross-references, and introduced 9. The project cost about US$1,400, roughly 2,500 times less than an estimate for the same work by lawyers. The substantive choices follow the 2027 programme of David Lisnard, but the project is an independent initiative and the method is policy-agnostic. All results are produced and measured by AI agents alone; we release them, with all texts, tools, samples and verdicts, for verification and hardening by legal experts and citizens.
