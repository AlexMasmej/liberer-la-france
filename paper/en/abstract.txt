French law is hard to read: a single rule is spread across a statute, its implementing decrees, the tax annexes and future consolidated versions, which often contradict one another. We report a proof of concept in which AI agents rewrite, and then adversarially audit, five major French codes in full (the Labour Code, the General Tax Code and its four annexes, the Social Security Code, the Town Planning Code, and the Construction and Housing Code): 30,317 article entries from the official LEGI database as of 18 October 2026. Two drafting passes by orchestrated coding agents produced 14,115 rewritten articles and 16,199 articles retained with a specific justification. A third pass, run as an independent red team, built a register of objections (303 raised, 243 retained on the final text), applied about 1,220 corrections through a guarded writer with before-image checks, triaged issues filed by an external contributor, and measured reliability with six blind, stratified random audits. On the same 1,879 randomly drawn articles, after checking each disputed defect against both texts, AI auditors find a gross defect in 7.0% ± 1.2 of the official texts in force but in 4.6% ± 0.9 of the rewritten law (paired test, p ≈ 2×10^-5): the rewrite repaired 77 defects of the official law, mostly dead or outdated cross-references, for 32 it introduced. Targeted sweeps fix the family they aim at but do not move the measured rate; a systematic chapter-by-chapter reading of the 2,740 rewritten tax articles found 96 gross defects (3.5%), 86 of which were corrected. The substantive choices follow the 2027 programme of David Lisnard, but the project is an independent initiative and the method is policy-agnostic. All results are produced and measured by AI agents alone; we release them, with all texts, tools, samples and verdicts, for verification and hardening by legal experts and citizens.
