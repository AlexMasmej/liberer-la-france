Turning a political programme into enforceable law means amending a vast, interconnected corpus: statutes, decrees, tax annexes and future versions that often contradict one another. We study whether a policy-configurable pipeline of AI agents can make this translation coherent, verifiable and far cheaper than manual drafting. Case study: five major French codes in full (Labour, the General Tax Code and its four annexes, Social Security, Town Planning, Construction and Housing), 30,317 articles from the official LEGI database as of 18 October 2026, rewritten according to reforms inspired by David Lisnard's 2027 programme, in an independent project. The work ran in three passes and an open public review: every article rewritten (14,115 rewritten, 16,199 retained with a justification), a complete coherence re-reading, then an independent red team that applied about 1,290 corrections and measured reliability with blind random audits. On two fresh samples totalling 1,264 articles, audited blind on both texts with the same protocol, the rewritten law has 54% fewer gross defects than the law in force (95% CI 40--64%): 3.5% ± 1.0 against 7.5% ± 1.5 (paired test, p ≈ 2×10⁻¹⁰). The cost is 2,500 to 10,000 times lower than estimates for the same work by lawyers. The programme is a separate input: defect detection does not depend on it, and we invite other programmes to be run through the same pipeline. These results, produced and measured by AI agents alone, are released for verification by lawyers and citizens.
