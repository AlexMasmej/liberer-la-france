French law is hard to read: a single rule is spread across a statute, its implementing decrees, the tax annexes and future consolidated versions, which often contradict one another. We report a proof of concept in which AI agents rewrite, and then adversarially audit, five major French codes in full (the Labour Code, the General Tax Code and its four annexes, the Social Security Code, the Town Planning Code, and the Construction and Housing Code): 30,317 article entries from the official LEGI database as of 18 October 2026. Two drafting passes produced 14,115 rewritten articles and 16,199 retained with a justification. A third pass, run as an independent red team, built a register of objections, applied about 1,290 corrections through a guarded writer, triaged issues filed by an external contributor, and measured reliability with six blind, stratified random audits. On two fresh random samples totalling 1,264 articles, audited blind on both texts with every disputed defect checked, AI auditors find a gross defect in 7.5% ± 1.5 of the official texts in force but in 3.5% ± 1.0 of the rewritten law today (paired test, p ≈ 2×10^-10): the rewrite repaired 60 defects of the official law, mostly dead cross-references, and introduced 9. A paired comparison on 1,879 articles agrees (7.0% against 4.6%). Targeted sweeps fix one defect family at a time without moving the measured rate; a systematic reading of the 2,740 rewritten tax articles found 96 gross defects and fixed 86. The project cost about US$1,400; the same work by lawyers would run to several million euros. The substantive choices follow the 2027 programme of David Lisnard, but the project is an independent initiative and the method is policy-agnostic. All results are produced and measured by AI agents alone; we release them, with all texts, tools, samples and verdicts, for verification and hardening by legal experts and citizens.
